@stage 0 | Sound Dojo | 音の道場 | First sounds, first words, first manners.
@day Five vowels and greetings | 母音とあいさつ
note: Japanese has five vowels: a (ah), i (ee), u (oo with relaxed lips), e (eh), o (oh). They never change, and each sound gets about the same length of time.
note: Bowing: a small nod works for everyday greetings. Bow from the waist, back straight, and look down rather than at the other person. Deeper and longer means more respect.
note: Which greeting? おはようございます until about 10 a.m., こんにちは through the day, こんばんは after dark. With family or close friends, おはよう alone is normal. さようなら sounds rather final, so people often say something else to friends.
new: @greet1
chat: greet1
homework: Say all five greetings out loud, three times each.
immerse: Greet yourself in the mirror in Japanese tomorrow morning.
material: 3. Greetings
@day Thanks and sorry | お礼とおわび
note: すみません is the all-rounder: it calls a waiter, says excuse me, and softens a thanks. ごめんなさい is a more personal "I'm sorry".
note: Hands together before a meal for いただきます is common at home. It is a thank you for the food. After eating, say ごちそうさまでした.

immerse: Say すみません to yourself whenever you bump into something.
material: 4. Manners
@day Who are you? | 自己紹介
note: Say your name, then です. People use family names and add さん to others. Never add さん to your own name.
note: Point at your own nose, not your chest, when you mean "me?". This is one common Japanese gesture.
new: @intro
chat: intro1
homework: Practise your introduction until you can say it without looking.
immerse: Label one object in your house with a sticky note.
material: Welcome!
@day Yes, no, and help | はい、いいえ
note: はい also means "I'm listening". Hearing it doesn't always mean agreement. Direct いいえ can sound blunt, so people may soften it with a wave of the hand in front of the face.
note: Listening noises (あいづち) such as はい and ええ show you're following. Silence can feel like you've stopped listening.

@stage 1 | Romaji Road | ローマ字の道 | Read and type Japanese sounds with letters.
@day Numbers one to five | 数字 一から五
note: Romaji is a bridge. It teaches you sounds and how to type, and you'll leave it behind soon.
note: Counting on fingers in Japan starts with an open palm, folding the fingers in from the thumb for one, two, three. Six onward opens them out again.
new: @num1
homework: Count aloud from one to five, ten times.
immerse: Count your steps on the stairs in Japanese today.
material: 5. Numbers
@day Numbers six to ten | 数字 六から十
note: Some numbers have two readings. 4 is よん or し, 7 is なな or しち, 9 is きゅう or く. し sounds like the word for death, and く can sound like suffering, so many people avoid them where they can.

immerse: Read any number you see today in Japanese.

@day Typing Japanese | 日本語を打つ
note: Typing is half of modern Japanese. Keyboards type romaji and turn it into kana. YomiGo converts for you in answer boxes, and you can also use the Windows or Android Japanese keyboard.
note: Set it up: Windows Settings > Time & language > Language & region > Add a language > Japanese. Switch with Alt + ` or the language icon. Use n twice (nn) for ん when in doubt.

note: Family words change depending on whose family. This is a preview: you'll meet the full polite set later.
note: Teachers are always 先生, used like a title: 田中先生. Don't use it for yourself.

@day Voiced sounds | 濁点
note: Two small dashes (dakuten) make a sound voiced: か ka becomes が ga. さ becomes ざ, た becomes だ, は becomes ば. じ and ぢ sound the same, as do ず and づ.

@day P sounds and small つ | 半濁点と促音
note: A small circle (handakuten) turns は row sounds to p: ぱ. A small っ means a tiny stop before the next sound. Type it by doubling the next consonant: kitte for きって.
new: %h.pa %h.pi %h.pu %h.pe %h.po @hwords12
homework: Clap one beat for each character in きっぷ: き, っ, ぷ, three beats.
immerse: Count beats in a Japanese word you hear today.
@day Combined sounds | 拗音
note: A small ゃ, ゅ, or ょ blends with the sound before it: き + ゃ = きゃ kya, in one beat. Keep them small on the page.
new: %h.kya %h.kyu %h.kyo %h.sha %h.shu %h.sho %h.cha %h.chu %h.cho %h.nya %h.nyu %h.nyo %h.hya %h.hyu %h.hyo %h.mya %h.myu %h.myo %h.rya %h.ryu %h.ryo %h.gya %h.gyu %h.gyo %h.ja %h.ju %h.jo %h.bya %h.byu %h.byo %h.pya %h.pyu %h.pyo @hwords13

@day Long vowels | 長音
note: Long vowels count as two beats, and length changes meaning: おばさん is aunt, おばあさん is grandmother. Long お is usually written おう, and long え usually えい.
