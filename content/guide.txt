# YomiGo's Study Guide. Written in YomiGo's own words, drawing on the guide sites listed in docs/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md.
@ Your setup checklist
Do these once, a little at a time. You don't need them all on day one.
1. Add the Japanese keyboard to your PC or phone.
2. Install YomiGo Reader in your browser, to look up words on any page.
3. Pick a regular study time, and a daily amount you can keep up on a bad day.
4. Find one easy show, game, or channel you enjoy.
5. After hiragana, consider Anki for your own flashcards, and Yomitan for a bigger dictionary.
6. Once you know some grammar, try a conversation partner on iTalki or HelloTalk.
@ What a good day looks like
Reviews first, every day, even if you skip the class.
Then one class at your chosen pace.
Then Japanese you enjoy: as little as 15 minutes at first, growing toward 1 to 2 hours a day as you can.
A common target once you're past the basics is about 10 new words and 2 grammar points a day. Expect around ten reviews for each new card.
@ If you fall behind
Lower your pace in Settings. Keep doing your reviews.
Nearly every guide agrees that new cards are the thing to cut. Skipped reviews are what make people give up.
Tired today? Watch or play something easy instead of doing nothing. It still counts.
@ Listening
Start listening early, before you understand it. You're learning the rhythm and sounds.
Active listening means you watch and pay attention. It teaches the most.
Passive listening means audio plays while you do chores. It fills gaps in your day.
When listening, go with the flow. Look up only words that stand out or keep coming back.
Beginners often do more listening than reading, roughly 7 parts to 3, moving toward an even split.
@ Reading
Start as soon as you can read kana, even if you understand little. Graded readers and kana-only games work well.
Reading is slower: read, look it up, read it again.
Most guides put reading at the centre of long-term progress. Vary what you read, or stick to everyday-life stories, which use the most useful words.
@ Sentence mining
Once you've finished the beginner stage, make your own flashcards from real Japanese. Save each word with the sentence you met it in.
YomiGo Reader does this in your browser, and exports to Anki.
Words you've met in something you care about stick far better than words from a list.
@ Saying I, you, he and she
わたし (私) works for everyone, in any situation. わたくし is more formal still, for work and speeches.
Casually, many men and boys say ぼく (僕), and おれ (俺) with friends, which sounds rough. Many women and girls say あたし.
Japanese leaves out "I" once it's clear, so you'll say it far less than in English.
"You" is mostly left out too. あなた can sound distant or cold, so use the person's name with さん: 田中さんは学生ですか。
きみ and おまえ are casual, and can sound rude to someone you don't know well.
かれ (彼) means he, and かのじょ (彼女) she, but they also mean boyfriend and girlfriend. Names are safer.
@ Grammar and kanji
Read grammar to understand it, not to memorise it. It won't stick the first time, and that's normal. If an explanation confuses you, try another guide.
Learn kanji through words. Guides warn against studying kanji only on their own for long. Mnemonics help when kanji look alike.
@ Mistakes to avoid
Rushing: too many new cards a day leads to burnout.
Skipping reviews.
Staying on beginner material too long, or waiting until you feel ready for real Japanese.
Polishing small details instead of moving on.
Relying on any one app alone, including this one. Real Japanese and real people are the goal.
@ Where the guides disagree
Handwriting: some say to practise kana by hand, and many say handwriting kanji is optional unless you want it. YomiGo has you write kana, and leaves kanji up to you.
Speaking: some leave it for later, and others warn against waiting. YomiGo has you speak from the start, because confidence grows with use.
Kanji courses: some like dedicated courses, others prefer words only. YomiGo uses small sets tied to words.
Language apps: several guides warn against relying on them. That's why YomiGo keeps sending you to real Japanese.
